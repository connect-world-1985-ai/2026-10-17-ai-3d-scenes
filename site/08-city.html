Part III — Recipes

Recipe: procedural city, night lights and fly-over

The "huge city at night, camera gliding between towers" clip is the signature AI-3D demo. It looks like it needs thousands of hand-made buildings. In practice it needs a street grid, one box geometry drawn thousands of times, windows painted by a shader, a sky colour that changes with the hour, a few hundred moving lights, bloom, and a smooth camera spline. This page builds exactly that, live.

The pipeline

  1. Street grid. The simplest is a regular grid of square blocks separated by streets. More organic layouts use L-systems that grow roads from a population-density map (Parish & Müller, SIGGRAPH 2001) or tensor fields. Verified
  2. Lots and buildings. Split each block into lots and give each lot a footprint and height. A downtown falloff (taller near the centre) and a rare "tower" multiplier give a skyline.
  3. Instancing. All buildings are one unit box drawn with per-instance scale, position and colour, so 3,000 buildings cost one draw call (page 05).
  4. Facades in the shader. Windows are not geometry: the fragment shader divides world position into 2.2 m × 3.2 m cells and paints a dark pane in each. A hash of the cell decides whether it is lit at night (page 04 shows the code).
  5. Life. Street lamps and cars are more instanced meshes; cars move along street lines, and the CPU rewrites their matrices each frame.
  6. Look. Day and night come from one sun-elevation value that drives sky colour, fog, light intensities, window emission, exposure and bloom strength together.

Car headlights are white instances heading one way and tail lights are red instances heading the other; at night they are pushed above 1.0 so bloom catches them. The sun casts one shadow map over the whole city. It gets blocky up close: the resolution-versus-coverage trade-off from page 03.

What makes the difference between "boxes" and "city"

Lighting and atmosphere give most of the gain; building geometry gives the least. This is my reading of the official examples and classic demos. Inference In order of payoff:

  1. Night. Darkness hides the simplicity of geometry; lit windows and street lights give scale and life. Pixel City (2009) was "mostly made of lights and suggestions", with no assets at all. Verified
  2. Fog and atmospheric perspective: distant buildings fade towards the sky colour, which reads as kilometres of depth.
  3. Height distribution: a believable skyline (downtown cluster, a few landmarks) beats random heights.
  4. Bloom on emissive windows, lamps and car lights.
  5. Variation: colour, roof heights, setbacks (stacked boxes), rooftop clutter (water tanks, AC units).
  6. Ground detail: sidewalks, lane markings, kerbs. This matters at street level, not from the air.
  7. Interior mapping (van Dongen 2008): fake rooms behind each window by raycasting virtual floors and walls in the shader. Cost is constant in the number of rooms. A strong upgrade for street-level views. Verified

Ready prompt (rung 3–4, browser)

Create a single index.html (no build step) using Three.js r186 via an import map from a CDN. Make a procedural night city: 12×12 blocks of 24 m with 8 m streets, each block split into 2×2 or 3×3 lots, building height growing towards the centre (downtown up to 150 m) with a seeded RNG (seed in the URL hash). All buildings in ONE InstancedMesh of a unit box with per-instance colour. Windows: inject GLSL with material.onBeforeCompile into MeshStandardMaterial: 2.2 m × 3.2 m cells from world position, dark glass, ~40% lit warm or cool at night via a hash of the cell index. Add instanced street lamps, 400 instanced cars moving along streets (white headlights one way, red tail lights the other). Time-of-day slider (0–24 h) driving sky colour, FogExp2 colour, sun, hemisphere light, window emission and bloom. Camera: a closed CatmullRomCurve3 fly-over between towers, plus an orbit mode. UnrealBloomPass + OutputPass, ACES tone mapping. HUD with draw calls, triangles and FPS. Keep draw calls under 50. Then open the page headlessly, check the console, screenshot at 13:00 and 21:00, and fix issues.
Typical failures in agent-made cities
  • Windows stretched or missing on some faces: computing cells in object space after instance scaling, or using the wrong axis per face. Fix: world-space position and the object normal to choose the axis.
  • Buildings overlap roads: placement without lots.
  • One draw call per building: a separate Mesh each; 5k buildings at 15 fps.
  • City vanishes at certain angles: a wrong bounding sphere on the InstancedMesh.
  • Everything glows: bloom threshold too low, so lit walls bloom as well as lights.
  • Camera clips through towers: a spline path not checked against building heights.
