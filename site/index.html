How AI agents make impressive interactive 3D scenes

You keep seeing a fly-over of a huge city, an ocean glittering under a low sun, a first-person walk down a street at night — and the caption says an agent made it "from one short prompt". This guide takes those demos apart: what the agent actually writes, which libraries and engines do the heavy lifting, where every pixel comes from, and how much of the "one prompt" story is true.

The short answer (details on every later page)
  • Most impressive browser demos are ordinary code. The agent writes JavaScript and shader code (usually Three.js), typically in one HTML file. The "realism" comes from well-known rendering techniques the model has seen thousands of times: physically based materials, an environment map, a sky model, tone mapping, fog and bloom. Inference
  • Some demos are not code at all: Google's photorealistic 3D tiles of a real city, a Gaussian-splat capture, an AI-generated world, or a plain AI video. They can look alike in a clip but are completely different things. Page 01 shows how to tell them apart.
  • "One prompt" usually means one first prompt. Where people publish their prompts and repositories, you find long briefs, many follow-up turns, a screenshot loop, and runs of one to several hours. A fixed-prompt benchmark (AA-3D, 2026-10-12) let agents work up to 3 hours or 250 turns per scene. Verified See page 12.
  • What matters most is the feedback loop: agents that take a screenshot of their own scene early do better. In AA-3D, how early a model first looks at its scene tracked its rank closely (Spearman 0.82). Verified See page 11.

About this research

This is an independent, vendor-neutral snapshot dated 2026-10-17. Six research passes gathered sourced fact sheets; the most important claims were re-checked directly (GitHub API star counts and licences, the AA-3D benchmark article, the night-street repository, Epic's City Sample post, Google's Map Tiles policies). Every claim carries an evidence tag:

  • Verified read in a primary source (docs, repository, paper, licence file, pricing page) on 2026-10-17;
  • Practice common engineering practice or a credible secondary source;
  • Inference my reasoning from verified facts;
  • Unverified a lead I could not confirm;
  • Experimental something the vendor itself labels experimental or beta.

Prices, star counts and model rankings age quickly. Method, sources and known gaps are on Sources and method.
