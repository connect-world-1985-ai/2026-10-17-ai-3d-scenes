Part V — Doing it

Decision framework, stacks and costs

Most people pick a tool first and a goal second. Reverse it. Five questions about what you want to deliver narrow the eight production routes to one or two. Then pick a stack that matches your experience, and budget money and time with the measured numbers at the bottom of this page.

Five questions

  1. Deliverable: a link, a video, or an app? This one question removes most routes.
  2. Real place or invented? Real places mean geodata, with its licence terms (page 10).
  3. Who must run it? Phones and integrated GPUs mean WebGL2 and tight budgets (page 13).
  4. How real must it look? "Stylised but polished" is cheap for an agent; "photoreal" costs hours and needs good assets or captures.
  5. How much will you iterate? If the answer is "not much", choose the route where the agent can see its output easily: the browser.

Stacks by experience

Beginner (first weekend)
One index.html, three.js pinned through an import map, CC0 assets from Poly Haven and Kenney, a coding agent (Claude Code, Codex or Cursor) plus a Playwright screenshot script. Host on GitHub Pages. Cost: your existing AI subscription.
Intermediate
Vite + three.js (or React Three Fiber), glTF pipeline with gltf-transform and KTX2, Rapier physics, Blender driven by headless Python scripts for asset preparation, deterministic video capture. Cost: still mostly free software.
Advanced
Unreal 5.8 with PCG and MCP (or Godot and Unity with MCP servers), Houdini for procedural assets, 3D tiles or splat captures, agent swarms with critics and persistent notes. Cost: a strong GPU, possible licences and API usage.
